A standard men's Olympic bar is 86.75 in, about 87 in. That bar does not fit in a 7-foot (84 in) room at all. In an 8-foot (96 in) room it leaves 4.625 in per side if you center it, and in a 9-foot (108 in) room it still leaves only 10.625 in per side. Those figures are below a practical 12 in per-side allowance for standing beside a sleeve to load plates.

The five short rackable bars in this catalog cut 14.75 in to 18.5 in off that length without giving up a shaft long enough, on the published numbers, to sit on common 46 in to 49 in racks. The tradeoff is sleeve length, and in one case, a 25 mm shaft.

The bars are the [Rogue C-70S](/products/rogue-c-70s-short-rackable-bar), [REP Colorado Short Bar](/products/rep-colorado-short-bar-15kg), [Bells of Steel Short Rackable Barbell](/products/bells-of-steel-short-rackable-barbell-69), [Fringe Sport Midas Mini Shorty Power Bar](/products/fringe-sport-midas-mini-shorty-power-bar), and [Rogue C-68S](/products/rogue-c-68s-womens-short-rackable-bar). Four are 28.5 mm shafts. The C-68S is the 25 mm women's bar.

## Room-width math

Overall lengths are the verified `overall_length_in` values: 72 in for the C-70S, Colorado Short, and Midas Mini; 69 in for the Bells of Steel; 68.25 in for the C-68S. Clearance below assumes the bar is centered and that leftover width is split evenly. The 12 in (tight) and 18 in (comfortable) per-side figures are working allowances for loading plates, not manufacturer specs. Collar length is not in this catalog, so leftover space is measured from bare overall length.

Compared with an 86.75 in bar, the 72 in bars are 14.75 in shorter, the 69 in bar is 17.75 in shorter, and the 68.25 in bar is 18.5 in shorter.

| Bar | Overall length | 7 ft room (84 in): leftover / per side | 8 ft room (96 in): leftover / per side | 9 ft room (108 in): leftover / per side |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional 86.75 in men's bar (not in this catalog) | 86.75 in | Does not fit (2.75 in too long) | 9.25 in / 4.625 in | 21.25 in / 10.625 in |
| [Rogue C-70S](/products/rogue-c-70s-short-rackable-bar), [REP Colorado Short](/products/rep-colorado-short-bar-15kg), [Fringe Midas Mini](/products/fringe-sport-midas-mini-shorty-power-bar) | 72 in | 12 in / 6 in | 24 in / 12 in | 36 in / 18 in |
| [Bells of Steel Short Rackable](/products/bells-of-steel-short-rackable-barbell-69) | 69 in | 15 in / 7.5 in | 27 in / 13.5 in | 39 in / 19.5 in |
| [Rogue C-68S](/products/rogue-c-68s-womens-short-rackable-bar) | 68.25 in | 15.75 in / 7.875 in | 27.75 in / 13.875 in | 39.75 in / 19.875 in |

**7-foot room.** Every short bar in this set physically fits. None of them gives 12 in per side if you leave the bar centered. A 72 in bar leaves 6 in per side. The 69 in and 68.25 in bars leave 7.5 in and 7.875 in. If you slide the bar flush to one wall to load one sleeve, the opposite side gets the full leftover: 12 in on a 72 in bar, 15 in on the 69 in bar, and 15.75 in on the C-68S. Plan to load one side, shift, then load the other.

**8-foot room.** This is where a short bar starts to pay off in both fit and loading. The 72 in bars hit exactly 12 in per side. The 69 in bar leaves 13.5 in, and the C-68S leaves 13.875 in. A conventional 86.75 in bar still leaves only 4.625 in per side.

**9-foot room.** The 72 in bars reach 18 in per side. The 69 in and 68.25 in bars leave 19.5 in and 19.875 in. A conventional 86.75 in bar still leaves 10.625 in per side, short of the 12 in tight allowance.

## Rackability: shaft length versus rack width

A short bar is rackable when the shaft between the sleeves is long enough to span the outside of the uprights, so the sleeves sit outside the J-cups. Cutting overall length only works if that length comes out of the sleeves, not the shaft.

Verified shaft lengths (distance between sleeves): [Rogue C-70S](/products/rogue-c-70s-short-rackable-bar) 52 in; [Bells of Steel](/products/bells-of-steel-short-rackable-barbell-69) 51.6 in; [REP Colorado Short](/products/rep-colorado-short-bar-15kg) and [Rogue C-68S](/products/rogue-c-68s-womens-short-rackable-bar) 51.5 in. Shaft length for the [Fringe Midas Mini](/products/fringe-sport-midas-mini-shorty-power-bar) is Not published. Fringe states that the bar "has the same shaft length of standard barbells" and calls it "a rackable, short-sleeve bar," but there is no numeric shaft length to run the clearance math.

The Bells of Steel 51.6 in figure comes from a [manufacturer blog](https://bellsofsteel.us/blogs/content/how-much-do-short-barbells-weigh), not from the product spec table. Treat it as manufacturer-published, but not table-verified on the product page.

Manufacturer rackability claims, in their words as recorded in the catalog notes:

- Rogue says the C-70S 52 in shaft and the C-68S 51.5 in shaft allow each bar to be racked on any well-known power racks, squat stands, or rigs.
- REP says the Colorado Short is "designed to be rackable on any standard 47" and 49" width racks."
- Bells of Steel says the bar "racks cleanly in any power rack up to 49" / 124.5 cm inside width."
- Fringe states that the Midas Mini is rackable, without a published shaft length.

Against this catalog, the useful comparison is shaft length minus a published upright span. Only racks with a clear outside-upright or overall-plus-inside width are used below. Margin is the shaft minus that published width, then split evenly.

| Rack | Published width used for the math | C-70S (52 in shaft) | Colorado Short and C-68S (51.5 in) | Bells of Steel (51.6 in) | Midas Mini (shaft Not published) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Rogue RML-3W](/products/rogue-rml-3w-fold-back-wall-mount-rack-21-5) | 49 in outside uprights (43 in inside) | 3 in total / 1.5 in per side past the outside uprights | 2.5 in / 1.25 in | 2.6 in / 1.3 in | Cannot calculate |
| [PRx Profile PRO](/products/prx-profile-pro-folding-squat-rack-89) | 48 in outside of uprights (53 in total rack width) | 4 in / 2 in | 3.5 in / 1.75 in | 3.6 in / 1.8 in | Cannot calculate |
| [REP PR-4100](/products/rep-pr-4100-folding-wall-mount-rack-21-5) | 48 in width (42.1 in inside width) | 4 in / 2 in past the published 48 in width | 3.5 in / 1.75 in | 3.6 in / 1.8 in | Cannot calculate |
| [Titan T-3 Folding Power Rack](/products/titan-t-3-series-folding-power-rack-82-21-5) | 46 in width (42 in inside width) | 6 in / 3 in past the published 46 in width | 5.5 in / 2.75 in | 5.6 in / 2.8 in | Cannot calculate |

On the tightest published outside span in that set, the RML-3W at 49 in, the 51.5 in shafts leave 1.25 in of shaft past each outside upright. That matches REP's "47 in and 49 in width racks" claim. The C-70S leaves 1.5 in per side on the same rack.

Two other catalog racks publish a width with no inside/outside split: the [Rogue HR-2](/products/rogue-hr-2-half-rack-90) at 48 in and the [Rogue SML-1](/products/rogue-sml-1-70-monster-lite-squat-stand) at 49 in. If those widths are the span the shaft must clear, the margins match the 48 in and 49 in rows above. The catalog does not label them as outside uprights, so do not treat that as confirmed.

Do not run the same subtraction against every `width_in` in the catalog. The [REP Apollo Half Rack](/products/rep-apollo-half-rack-80) is 52.4 in wide, and the [REP PR-1100](/products/rep-pr-1100-power-rack) is 58.1 in wide with a 44 in working width. Neither record publishes an outside-upright span. The [Titan T-2](/products/titan-t-2-series-power-rack-71) and [Titan T-3 Short Squat Stand](/products/titan-t-3-series-short-squat-stand) have no published width (footprints and inside or walk-in dimensions are given without width/depth axis labels).

One wording conflict to take at face value: Bells of Steel claims compatibility up to a 49 in *inside* width, while its recorded shaft is 51.6 in. This catalog has no rack with a published 49 in inside width. The 49 in figures here are the RML-3W's outside uprights and the SML-1's unlabeled width. The widest published inside widths in the table above are 43 in (RML-3W), 42.1 in (PR-4100), and 42 in (Titan T-3 Folding). On those inside figures, a 51.6 in shaft clears. A rack that truly measured 49 in inside, with 3x3 in uprights, would have a larger outside span than 51.6 in, and this catalog cannot confirm that case.

## Loadable sleeves, weight, and construction

Shortening a rackable bar usually comes out of the sleeves. Verified loadable sleeve lengths: Colorado Short 8.9 in (longest in this set), C-70S 8.875 in, Bells of Steel 7.88 in (also stated as 7-7/8 in / 200 mm), C-68S 7.75 in (shortest verified). Loadable sleeve length on the Midas Mini is Not published. Fringe's spec table lists "Sleeve Length 9 in" without saying that figure is loadable length, so it is not used as a loadable measurement here.

That is a real capacity tradeoff even before you look at published ratings. The 69 in and 68.25 in bars give you more room-width clearance and give up about an inch of loadable sleeve versus the 72 in bars that publish a sleeve length. Plate thickness is not in this catalog, so this guide does not convert sleeve length into a plate count.

Published bar weights: Colorado Short 33.1 lb (15 kg); Midas Mini 33 lb (15 kg); Bells of Steel 29 lb (13 kg); C-68S 25 lb. C-70S bare-bar weight is Not published. Rogue states the C-70S "weighs 15KG only with collars included." Do not treat 15 kg as the bare bar.

Published static ratings: Colorado Short 1,500 lb and Midas Mini 1,500 lb; Bells of Steel 900 lb / 408 kg static. Rogue does not publish static weight ratings for its barbells. The C-70S carries an F-Rating of F16-R, and the C-68S carries F12-R. Tensile strength is 190,000 psi on every bar except Bells of Steel, which is 150,000 psi. REP also publishes a 1% tolerance on the Colorado Short.

Shaft diameter is 28.5 mm on the C-70S, Colorado Short, Bells of Steel, and Midas Mini, and 25 mm on the C-68S. Sleeve diameter is 1.97 in on the Colorado Short, converted from a published 50 mm; it is Not published on the other four.

The Bells of Steel is the only bar with a verified center knurl. The C-70S, Colorado Short, and C-68S have none. Center knurl on the Midas Mini is Not published. All five use bushings, not bearings, and all five publish dual knurl marks.

## Buy this / skip that

**Buy the [Rogue C-70S](/products/rogue-c-70s-short-rackable-bar) if** you want the longest verified shaft in this set (52 in), a 72 in 28.5 mm bar, 8.875 in loadable sleeves, 190,000 psi, dual IWF and IPF marks, no center knurl, and composite bushings, and you are fine without a published bare-bar weight or static rating. It leaves 12 in per side in an 8-foot room and 1.5 in of shaft past each side of a 49 in outside-upright rack.

**Skip the C-70S if** you need a published bare-bar weight, a published static capacity, a 25 mm shaft, or more than 12 in of leftover room width in an 8-foot space. A 72 in bar still leaves only 6 in per side in a 7-foot room if centered.

**Buy the [REP Colorado Short Bar](/products/rep-colorado-short-bar-15kg) if** you want the most complete published spec sheet in this catalog, a verified 33.1 lb / 15 kg 28.5 mm bar, the longest loadable sleeves (8.9 in), the only published sleeve diameter (1.97 in / 50 mm), a 1,500 lb static rating, 190,000 psi, and an explicit 47 in and 49 in rack-width claim. Shaft length is 51.5 in. Room-width math is the same as the other 72 in bars.

**Skip the Colorado Short if** you need a center knurl, a shorter-than-72 in bar, or a 25 mm shaft.

**Buy the [Bells of Steel Short Rackable Barbell](/products/bells-of-steel-short-rackable-barbell-69) if** you want the only 28.5 mm option shorter than 72 in (69 in overall), the only verified center knurl in this set, and a 29 lb / 13 kg bar, and a 900 lb static weight capacity with 150,000 psi tensile strength is enough for your lifts. In an 8-foot room it leaves 13.5 in per side, 1.5 in more than the 72 in bars. The 51.6 in shaft leaves 1.3 in past each side of a 49 in outside-upright span.

**Skip the Bells of Steel if** you need 190,000 psi, a 1,500 lb-class published rating, longer loadable sleeves, or a shaft length taken from the product spec table rather than the manufacturer blog.

**Buy the [Fringe Sport Midas Mini](/products/fringe-sport-midas-mini-shorty-power-bar) if** you want a 72 in, 33 lb / 15 kg, 28.5 mm bar with a 1,500 lb rating and 190,000 psi, and you can accept that shaft length, loadable sleeve length, sleeve diameter, and center knurl are all Not published. Fringe calls it rackable and says the shaft matches a standard barbell, but this catalog cannot compute rack clearance or sleeve capacity from a number.

**Skip the Midas Mini if** you need a verified shaft length or loadable sleeve length to do the fit math yourself, or if the unresolved chrome-versus-titanium finish wording on the product page bothers you.

**Buy the [Rogue C-68S](/products/rogue-c-68s-womens-short-rackable-bar) if** you want a 25 mm shaft and the shortest overall length in this set (68.25 in). That is the best published room-width result: 7.875 in per side in a 7-foot room, 13.875 in in an 8-foot room, and 19.875 in in a 9-foot room. Shaft length is 51.5 in, the same as the Colorado Short, so the rack-width margins match that bar. Weight is a published 25 lb. Tensile strength is 190,000 psi. Bushings are bronze.

**Skip the C-68S if** you want a 28.5 mm shaft, more than 7.75 in of loadable sleeve, a published static capacity, or a center knurl.
