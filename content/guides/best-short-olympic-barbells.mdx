## Quick verdict

A standard 86.75 in men's Olympic bar does not fit a 7 ft (84 in) room.

In 8 ft and 9 ft rooms it still leaves less than a practical 12 in per side for loading plates.

These five short rackable bars cut 14.75 in to 18.5 in off that length. Published shaft lengths still clear common 46 in to 49 in racks.

The tradeoff is sleeve length, and in one case a 25 mm shaft.

- **Best overall 72 in bar:** The [REP Colorado Short](/products/rep-colorado-short-bar-15kg) has the most complete published specs, including 33.1 lb / 15 kg weight, 8.9 in loadable sleeves, and a 1,500 lb static rating.
- **Longest verified shaft:** The [Rogue C-70S](/products/rogue-c-70s-short-rackable-bar) has a 52 in shaft and 8.875 in loadable sleeves. Bare-bar weight and static capacity are not published.
- **Best 28.5 mm bar under 72 in:** The [Bells of Steel Short Rackable](/products/bells-of-steel-short-rackable-barbell-69) is 69 in and 29 lb, with the only verified center knurl in this set.
- **Bells of Steel tradeoffs:** Tensile strength is 150,000 psi; static rating is 900 lb. The 51.6 in shaft comes from a manufacturer blog, not the product spec table.
- **Shortest overall length:** The [Rogue C-68S](/products/rogue-c-68s-womens-short-rackable-bar) is a 68.25 in, 25 lb, 25 mm women's bar with the same 51.5 in shaft as the Colorado Short.
- **72 in option with gaps:** The [Fringe Sport Midas Mini](/products/fringe-sport-midas-mini-shorty-power-bar) is a 33 lb / 15 kg, 28.5 mm bar with a 1,500 lb rating.
- **Midas Mini gaps:** Shaft length and loadable sleeve length are not published.

The 12 in (tight) and 18 in (comfortable) per-side figures below are working allowances, not manufacturer specs. Collar length is not published, so leftover space is measured from bare overall length.

## Room-width math

Clearance assumes the bar is centered and leftover width is split evenly.

| Bar | Overall length | 7 ft (84 in) leftover / side | 8 ft (96 in) leftover / side | 9 ft (108 in) leftover / side |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional men's bar | 86.75 in | Does not fit | 9.25 in / 4.625 in | 21.25 in / 10.625 in |
| [Rogue C-70S](/products/rogue-c-70s-short-rackable-bar) | 72 in | 12 in / 6 in | 24 in / 12 in | 36 in / 18 in |
| [REP Colorado Short](/products/rep-colorado-short-bar-15kg) | 72 in | 12 in / 6 in | 24 in / 12 in | 36 in / 18 in |
| [Fringe Midas Mini](/products/fringe-sport-midas-mini-shorty-power-bar) | 72 in | 12 in / 6 in | 24 in / 12 in | 36 in / 18 in |
| [Bells of Steel](/products/bells-of-steel-short-rackable-barbell-69) | 69 in | 15 in / 7.5 in | 27 in / 13.5 in | 39 in / 19.5 in |
| [Rogue C-68S](/products/rogue-c-68s-womens-short-rackable-bar) | 68.25 in | 15.75 in / 7.875 in | 27.75 in / 13.875 in | 39.75 in / 19.875 in |

- **7 ft conventional bar:** An 86.75 in bar is 2.75 in too long for an 84 in room.
- **7 ft loading trap:** Every short bar here fits, but none leaves 12 in per side if you keep it centered.
- **How to load in 7 ft:** Plan to load one sleeve, shift, then load the other.
- **8 ft and 9 ft:** A short bar pays off for loading here. A conventional bar still leaves 4.625 in per side in 8 ft and 10.625 in in 9 ft.

## Rackability: shaft length versus rack width

A short bar is rackable when the shaft between the sleeves spans the outside of the uprights. Cutting overall length only works if that length comes out of the sleeves, not the shaft.

- **Verified shaft lengths:** [Rogue C-70S](/products/rogue-c-70s-short-rackable-bar) 52 in; [Bells of Steel](/products/bells-of-steel-short-rackable-barbell-69) 51.6 in; [REP Colorado Short](/products/rep-colorado-short-bar-15kg) and [Rogue C-68S](/products/rogue-c-68s-womens-short-rackable-bar) 51.5 in.
- **Midas Mini shaft:** Not published. Fringe says it "has the same shaft length of standard barbells" and calls it "a rackable, short-sleeve bar," but gives no number for the math.
- **Bells of Steel 51.6 in:** This figure comes from a [manufacturer blog](https://bellsofsteel.us/blogs/content/how-much-do-short-barbells-weigh), not from the product spec table.

Manufacturer rackability claims:

- Rogue says the C-70S 52 in shaft and the C-68S 51.5 in shaft allow each bar to be racked on any well-known power racks, squat stands, or rigs.
- REP says the Colorado Short is "designed to be rackable on any standard 47" and 49" width racks."
- Bells of Steel says the bar "racks cleanly in any power rack up to 49" / 124.5 cm inside width."
- Fringe states that the Midas Mini is rackable, without a published shaft length.

Margin below is shaft length minus a published upright span, then split evenly. Only racks with a clear outside-upright or overall-plus-inside width are used.

- **Tightest published span:** On the RML-3W's 49 in outside uprights, the 51.5 in shafts leave 1.25 in past each side. That matches REP's 47 in and 49 in rack claim.
- **Unlabeled widths:** The [Rogue HR-2](/products/rogue-hr-2-half-rack-90) is 48 in and the [Rogue SML-1](/products/rogue-sml-1-70-monster-lite-squat-stand) is 49 in, with no inside/outside label. Do not treat those as confirmed outside spans.
- **Do not force the math:** The [REP Apollo](/products/rep-apollo-half-rack-80) is 52.4 in wide, and the [REP PR-1100](/products/rep-pr-1100-power-rack) is 58.1 in wide with a 44 in working width. Neither publishes an outside-upright span.
- **Unlabeled Titan bases:** The [Titan T-2](/products/titan-t-2-series-power-rack-71) and [Titan T-3 Short Squat Stand](/products/titan-t-3-series-short-squat-stand) have no published width. Inside or walk-in figures are given without width/depth axis labels.
- **Fit trap:** Bells of Steel claims racks up to a 49 in inside width, but its published shaft is 51.6 in. This guide has no rack with a 49 in inside width.
- **Inside widths that do clear:** The widest published inside widths above are 43 in (RML-3W), 42.1 in (PR-4100), and 42 in (Titan T-3 Folding). A 51.6 in shaft clears those.
